# EP02 — Find a Problem Worth Solving
Extracted verbatim from MASTER_PRODUCTION_BRIEF.md §9. Not in production.

## Title

**Find a Problem Worth Solving**

## Target runtime

6:00.

## Core question

How do you identify a problem that is important enough for people to change their behaviour or pay for a solution?

## Narrative purpose

Move from Andrew's personal experience selling pigs into a repeatable method for finding problems. The episode should teach viewers how to investigate problems before deciding to build software.

## Main points

1. Start with a problem you have experienced or understand.
2. Identify a specific audience rather than “everyone”.
3. Look for existing workarounds.
4. Understand what happens when the problem occurs.
5. Separate a mild inconvenience from a costly or urgent problem.
6. Ask about real past behaviour, not hypothetical enthusiasm.
7. Consider whether software is actually the right solution.
8. Distinguish a one-sided SaaS product from a two-sided marketplace.
9. End with a practical problem-discovery exercise.

## Episode 2 visual direction

Use the same A–E design language:
- A: strong typography for the central questions.
- B: problem-discovery framework, problem-cost matrix, workaround map, and one-sided vs two-sided product diagram.
- C: abstract software metaphors only when explaining how a problem might become a product.
- D: original illustrations of people experiencing practical problems.
- E: case-file exhibits showing “Problem”, “Workaround”, “Cost”, “Frequency”, and “Evidence”.

## Episode 2 opening hook

“Before you build an app, you need to answer one question: are you solving a problem people actually care about?”

## Episode 2 ending

End with the lesson that identifying a problem is only the first step. The next episode will explain how to validate demand before building.
